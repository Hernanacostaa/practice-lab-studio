Amazon / Internal tool & rollout

Less friction at ticket submission.

I built an internal ticketing tool and expanded it from a pilot to multiple buildings. Frontline feedback informed three major versions, so the work wasn’t finished at the first release.

Read the tool and rollout story

The problem. Ticket submission was more time-consuming than it needed to be. I focused on that everyday workflow rather than creating a separate destination for its own sake.

My contribution. I personally built the tool, rolled it out across sites, and used frontline feedback to inform major revisions.

The choice. Start with a pilot, then keep revising as the tool reached more buildings. The supported account is feedback-led iteration; it doesn’t establish a particular feature change or experiment.

Delivered. A working internal tool and a multi-site rollout. This account describes historical use, not current usage or a claim of financial savings.

Technical detail. JavaScript and HTML/CSS, hosted with Amazon S3 and CloudFront. No internal interface or source code is reproduced here.

Microsoft / AI-assisted authoring

A draft a trainer could actually use.

Learning requirements and operating procedures still needed to become a trainer guide. I built the AI-assisted workflow, then worked on the structure, review, and editable document handoff.

Read the authoring story

The problem. Having the source material wasn’t the same as having a guide another trainer could follow.

My contribution. I built the Copilot Studio and Power Automate workflow, designed prompts and document fields, and partnered with learning program managers and engineers on needs, scope, risks, and output quality.

The choice. Keep the draft grounded in its sources, structured, and editable rather than treating a chat response as the finished document.

Delivered. A working version I shared and demonstrated, followed by a production-oriented redesign. The public demo makes editing, review, and export inspectable; it isn’t evidence of the original implementation’s exact controls or measured results.

Microsoft / Global curriculum leadership

Training that didn’t need me in every room.

I coordinated training across sites and prepared secondary trainers with reusable materials and train-the-trainer programs. Local delivery depended on more than a good slide deck.

Read the program delivery story

The problem. New technology brought learning needs across regions, with equipment, scheduling, and operational constraints to work around.

My contribution. I coordinated delivery, worked alongside engineers and program managers, and created hands-on guides and train-the-trainer materials to support regional trainers.

The delivery approach. Reusable guidance and secondary trainers gave other teams a way to carry the training forward locally. That describes the program design, not a measured improvement in staff readiness.

Scope and limits. Multi-site training and trainer preparation are delivery scope. They don’t establish faster onboarding, productivity gains, or financial impact.

Mobility and new technology. A global rapid-response stretch assignment alongside my formal training role also brought field experience and operational constraints into the work.
