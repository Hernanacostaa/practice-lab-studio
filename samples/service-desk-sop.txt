S1 / Intake fields and practice reports
FICTIONAL ENGINEERING SOP: APPLICATION-INCIDENT INTAKE
Original public portfolio example. Owner: Fictional service-platform engineer. Document: SOP-DEMO-IT-01. Revision: 1.0. Source-set date: 2026-11-01.
Scope: document and route a software issue report. The exercise does not include diagnosis, repair, or closure.
Required supplied facts: ticket ID, reporter alias, reported time, service, symptom, affected group, workaround availability, and whether work is blocked. All aliases, services, queues, reports, and times below are synthetic.
T1 | reporter Cedar | time 09:10 exercise time | service ScheduleBoard | symptom: the schedule page will not open | affected group: one team | workaround: none | work blocked: yes.
T2 | reporter Indigo | time 09:20 exercise time | service FileShelf | symptom: the upload button is unavailable | affected group: one user | workaround: an alternative upload path is available | work blocked: no.
T3 | reporter Amber | time 09:30 exercise time | service ScheduleBoard | symptom: the schedule page is slow | affected group: not supplied | workaround: not supplied | work blocked: not supplied.
No root cause, actual employee details, account data, service restoration, or resolution is supplied.

S2 / Classification, priority matrix, and queues
All reports in S1 belong to the fictional category Application incident. Preserve the reported symptom without adding a diagnosis.
Impact: one team is Team; one user is Individual. Other or missing scope needs clarification.
Urgency: no workaround AND work blocked is High; a workaround available AND work not blocked is Normal. Other or missing combinations need clarification.
Fictional priority matrix:
Team + High = P1.
Team + Normal = P2.
Individual + High = P2.
Individual + Normal = P3.
Service-to-queue mapping: ScheduleBoard -> Schedule Support; FileShelf -> File Support. These are fictional practice queues, not real destinations.
When all required facts and rules are supported, record Ready for practice routing with category, impact, urgency, priority, and the mapped queue. Hand the intake card to the trainer, not a real service desk.
When required facts are missing, record Needs clarification, preserve known facts, and write the specific follow-up question. Leave priority and destination unassigned until clarification, even when a service-to-queue mapping is known. Do not infer unknown values.
The matrix and completeness gate are fictional local policy, not a universal ITIL or Atlassian rule. No response-time SLA or recovery steps are specified.

S3 / Materials and record controls
Use printed or locally displayed T1-T3 reports, the S2 matrix and queue map, a blank intake card covering the required facts and derived decisions, and a pencil or local text editor. Include spaces for outcome and clarification questions.
Keep original reports unchanged. All activity ends with a card handed to the trainer. No ticketing account, service login, terminal, external messaging, or personal data is needed. The training allocation and learner requirements are supplied separately by the L&D assessment.

S4 / Exercise boundaries
Simulation only: use fictional tickets and queues. No real personal data, accounts, credentials, technical commands, production changes, or messages.
Stop if live services, real tickets, or operational actions are introduced; return to fictional materials.
Do not troubleshoot, reset an account, diagnose a cause, send a real escalation, or mark a service restored. A completed intake card is not incident resolution.
