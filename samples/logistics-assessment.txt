Audience: New receiving clerks moving from paper summaries to a consistent shipment-record workflow. Prerequisite: read a record and compare identifiers and quantities.
This example assumes that learners can read labels but have not learned the distinction between a logistics-unit ID and a product ID, or what to do when shipment records disagree. It does not claim interviews, observed errors, or adoption results. Repeated receipt processing across sites makes a common checking method useful.

A2 / Required knowledge and skills
Learners need to explain that an SSCC identifies a logistics unit, while a product ID identifies its contents. An advance ship notice (ASN) describes the expected shipment before receipt.
They must locate the ASN entry by the exact unit ID, compare the product ID, quantity, and unit of measure, and distinguish an actual match from a discrepancy or insufficient information.
They must record the supplied facts and use the designated exception route instead of assuming a conversion, changing the expected record, or accepting a shipment. These are data-comparison skills, not goods-handling skills.

A3 / Observable completion requirements
Each learner must correctly handle all four records supplied by the engineering SOP: a match, a quantity discrepancy, a unit-of-measure ambiguity, and an identifier absent from the ASN. Their comparison card must show both expected and reported values, the exercise outcome, and any required practice recipient or clarification question.
They must explain why a product ID cannot replace the unit ID and why a flagged exception can be the correct result. The trainer observes individual performance; a missed requirement means feedback and another attempt. Completion applies only to this fictional exercise, not warehouse qualification or real receipt approval.
